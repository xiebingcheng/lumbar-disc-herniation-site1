Symptoms & Diagnosis

Lumbar disc herniation symptoms range from mild discomfort to severe, disabling pain. Understanding your symptoms helps determine the urgency and type of treatment needed.

Common Symptoms

⚡

Lower Back Pain

Acute or chronic pain in the lumbar region. Worsens with sitting, coughing, sneezing, or forward bending. May be accompanied by muscle spasms.

🔥

Sciatica (Radiculopathy)

Burning, shooting, or shock-like pain radiating from the lower back through the buttock and down the leg. Often affects one side. The hallmark sign of nerve compression.

🦶

Numbness & Tingling

Pins and needles sensation (paresthesia) in the lower back, leg, foot, or toes. Indicates irritated or compressed nerves.

💪

Muscle Weakness

Difficulty lifting the foot (foot drop), weakness when walking on heels or toes, or general leg weakness affecting mobility and balance.

🦵

Straight Leg Raise Pain

Pain triggered when raising a straight leg while lying down. A classic diagnostic sign of lumbar disc herniation.

😰

Reflex Changes

Reduced or absent ankle reflexes, weakened knee reflex, or sensory deficits in specific dermatomes indicating nerve involvement.

Warning Signs (Seek Immediate Care)

⚠️ Cauda Equina Syndrome — Emergency

Seek emergency medical attention immediately if you experience:

  • Loss of bladder or bowel control
  • Severe or progressive leg weakness
  • Saddle anesthesia (numbness in inner thighs, buttocks, back of legs)
  • Sudden onset of neurological symptoms

Severity Classification

Mild

Early Stage

Intermittent back pain, occasional tingling. Conservative treatment often effective. Daily activities may be minimally affected.

Moderate

Progressive

Consistent sciatica, noticeable weakness, pain affecting sleep and daily activities. May still respond to conservative treatment or require minimally invasive intervention.

Severe

Advanced

Significant weakness, constant pain, difficulty walking. Surgical intervention typically recommended for best outcomes.

Emergency

Cauda Equina

Bladder/bowel dysfunction, saddle anesthesia, severe weakness. Requires emergency surgery within 24-48 hours to prevent permanent damage.

Diagnosis Process

Step 1

Medical History

Your surgeon asks about symptom onset, triggers, duration, previous treatments tried, and impact on quality of life.

Step 2

Physical Examination

Neurological assessment including reflexes, muscle strength testing, sensation evaluation, and specific provocative tests (straight leg raise, etc.).

Step 3

MRI Scan

The gold standard for diagnosing disc herniation. Our 3T MRI provides high-resolution images of disc integrity, nerve compression, and spinal cord status.

Step 4

Treatment Recommendation

Based on findings, your surgeon recommends the most appropriate treatment path—conservative management or surgical intervention.
