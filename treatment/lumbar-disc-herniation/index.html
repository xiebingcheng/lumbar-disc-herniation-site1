Lumbar Disc Herniation Treatment Abroad

Lumbar disc herniation occurs when the soft center of a spinal disc pushes through a tear in the tougher exterior casing, pressing on nerves and causing pain, numbness, or weakness in the legs. When conservative treatments fail, surgery offers the fastest path to relief.

Symptoms & When to Seek Treatment

⚡

Lower Back Pain

Acute or chronic pain in the lumbar region, often worsening with sitting, coughing, or sneezing.

🔥

Sciatica

Radiating pain from the lower back down through the buttock, thigh, and leg—often described as burning or sharp.

🦶

Numbness/Tingling

Pins and needles sensation in the legs, feet, or toes, indicating nerve compression.

💪

Muscle Weakness

Difficulty lifting the foot (foot drop) or weakness in the legs affecting walking or balance.

Warning signs requiring immediate attention: Progressive leg weakness, loss of bladder or bowel control (cauda equina syndrome), or severe, unrelenting pain.

Diagnosis Process

Step 1

Medical History Review

Your surgeon reviews your symptoms, duration, previous treatments, and impact on daily life.

Step 2

Physical Examination

Neurological tests to assess reflexes, sensation, muscle strength, and range of motion.

Step 3

MRI Imaging

Gold-standard imaging to visualize disc herniation, nerve compression, and spinal cord status. Our 3T MRI provides exceptional clarity.

Step 4

Treatment Plan

Surgeon recommends the most appropriate treatment based on findings and your lifestyle goals.

Treatment Options

💊

Conservative Treatment

Physical therapy, anti-inflammatory medications, epidural steroid injections, and activity modification. Effective for many patients.

🔬

Microdiscectomy

Minimally invasive removal of herniated disc material pressing on nerves. 1-2 hour surgery, same-day walking, rapid recovery.

🎯

Endoscopic Discectomy

Smallest incision technique using camera-guided laser. Fastest recovery, minimal tissue damage, tiny scar.

⚙️

Spinal Fusion

For severe cases with instability. Advanced implants provide stability. Longer recovery but permanent solution.

🦴

Artificial Disc Replacement

Motion-preserving alternative to fusion. Removes damaged disc, replaces with functional implant. Maintains spine mobility.

Recovery Timeline

Week 1-2

Walking within 24 hours post-surgery. Light activities resume. No bending, lifting, or twisting. Incision care instructions provided.

Week 3-4

Return to desk work. Driving allowed if pain-free. Start gentle physical therapy exercises. Walking program 20-30 minutes daily.

Week 6-8

Most patients return to light physical work. Low-impact exercise approved. Strengthening exercises begin under therapist guidance.

Month 3-6

Full activity typically resumed. Fusion patients may need 6-12 months for full bone healing. Follow-up MRI confirms healing progress.
