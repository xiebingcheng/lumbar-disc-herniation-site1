Lumbar disc herniation is a common spinal condition that affects millions of people worldwide. When the soft center of a spinal disc pushes through a tear in its outer layer, it can press on nearby nerves, causing pain, numbness, and weakness.

## What Causes a Herniated Disc?

The most common causes include:

- **Age-related degeneration** — Discs lose water content and flexibility over time
- **Physical stress** — Heavy lifting, repetitive motions, or improper lifting technique
- **Trauma** — Accidents or sudden impacts
- **Genetics** — Some people are more prone to disc problems
- **Smoking** — Reduces blood flow to spinal discs

## Symptoms to Watch For

Not everyone with a herniated disc experiences symptoms. When they do occur, common signs include:

- Lower back pain
- Pain radiating down the leg (sciatica)
- Numbness or tingling in legs or feet
- Muscle weakness
- Pain worsened by specific movements

## When to See a Doctor

If your symptoms persist for more than a few weeks, worsen, or significantly impact your daily life, it's time to consult a spine specialist. Seek immediate medical attention if you experience bladder or bowel control issues or severe leg weakness.

## Diagnosis

MRI is the gold standard for diagnosing lumbar disc herniation. Your doctor may also recommend CT scans or neurological tests to assess nerve function.

## Treatment Options

Treatment ranges from conservative approaches (physical therapy, medications, injections) to surgical options (microdiscectomy, endoscopic discectomy, spinal fusion). The right treatment depends on your specific condition and severity.
